Sundarbans: Kingdom of the Royal Bengal Tiger

The Complete 2026 Safari, Mangrove Houseboat & Watchtower Expedition Guide
📍 South 24 Parganas, West Bengal • ⏱️ 14 Min Comprehensive Read • ✍️ Curated by Bibek Mahata

🌿 The Living Mangrove Labyrinth

Spanning over 10,000 square kilometers across the lower Gangetic delta of India and Bangladesh, the Sundarbans Mangrove Delta is the largest contiguous halophytic mangrove forest on Earth. A UNESCO World Heritage Site and Ramsar Wetland, this saline wilderness is shaped by tidal ebbs and flows, creating an ever-shifting maze of waterways, mudflats, and dense Sundari trees.

The Sundarbans is world-famous as the home of the Royal Bengal Tiger (*Panthera tigris tigris*) — the only tiger population globally adapted to swimming across broad tidal rivers and hunting in mangrove ecosystems.

🐊 High-Biodiversity Checklist: Beyond tigers, keep your binoculars focused on Saltwater Crocodiles basking on muddy banks, endangered Gangetic & Irrawaddy river dolphins, spotted chital deer, water monitor lizards, and over 260 species of exotic birds (including 9 varieties of kingfishers).

🛥️ Key Watchtowers & Boat Safari Routes

All wildlife exploration in the Indian Sundarbans is conducted by licensed motorized river boats departing primarily from Godkhali Ghat. Key watchtower destinations include:

  • Sajnekhali Watchtower: The administrative gateway of the Sundarbans Tiger Reserve, featuring an interpretive museum, sweet-water drinking pond, and the Mangrove Crocodile Rehabilitation Center.
  • Sudhanyakhali Watchtower: Considered the highest-probability vantage point for tiger sightings, overlooking a freshwater watering hole frequented by deer and wild boars.
  • Dobanki Canopy Walk: A thrilling 496-meter elevated walkway suspended 20 feet above the forest floor, protected by heavy-duty iron netting allowing visitors to peer straight into the dense mangrove canopy.
  • Burir Dabri & Netidhopani: Deep-forest watchtowers preserving the ruins of a 400-year-old historic Shiva temple and offering vistas into the core reserve zone.

🚆 How to Reach Godkhali Ghat from Kolkata

  • By Local Train (Budget Friendly): Board any morning Canning Local from Sealdah South Station (approx. 1 hr 15 mins, ₹15 fare). From Canning station, shared autos and private magic-vans take you to Godkhali Ghat in 45 minutes.
  • By Road / Private Car: Drive from Kolkata via the Basanti Highway (EM Bypass ➔ Ghatakpukur ➔ Malancha ➔ Godkhali). Distance is approx. 85 km (~3 hours). Parking facilities are available at Godkhali Jetty.
  • Permits & Entry: Foreign tourists require official forest permits issued at Sajnekhali Range Office with passport and valid Indian e-Visa.
